I treated every culinary misstep as a personal failure, a reflection of some fundamental inadequacy.

But now, I’ve learned to taste and sit with it. To appreciate it as it is, even when it falls short of ideal. Life operates on the same principle, I’ve discovered. Some days arrive bland and uninspiring. Others overwhelm with their intensity. But even in imperfection, there is richness. Even in imbalance, there is flavor worth savoring. Presence matters more than perfection.

Salt also taught me about irreversibility. Unlike most ingredients, you cannot remove salt once added. There’s no going back, no undoing. This lesson has profound implications beyond the kitchen. Some words, once spoken, cannot be unspoken. Some decisions, once made, cannot be unmade. Salt taught me to act with intention, to taste along the way, to proceed with both confidence and care.
