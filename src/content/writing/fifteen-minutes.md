I had gone for a passport picture. A simple errand. But somehow, those fifteen minutes folded time like a well-kept sari.

The lights were still harsh. The stool still wobbled. The white backdrop was still slightly stained at the corners. And the photographer still said, “Chin up. No smile. Just relax,” as if that exact combination had ever worked on anyone. I sat there, stiff in my shirt, blinking too often, thinking too hard about how not to look like I was thinking.

It brought me back to the first time I ever sat in front of that white sheet. I was five. My mother had braided my hair so tight I couldn’t move my forehead. My shoes didn’t touch the floor. The studio smelled of film rolls and talcum powder.
