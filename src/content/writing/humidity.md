Your eyeliner smudges.
Your hair frizzes.
Your patience thins.

And suddenly, everyone around you becomes… real.

The person on the metro dabbing their forehead with a wrinkled napkin. The mother in the park trying to soothe a cranky toddler while juggling a half-spilled juice box. The couple walking together but holding hands at arm’s length for a breeze that never arrives.

In that stickiness and surrender, I began to see something I hadn’t before: undeniable humanness.

Humidity makes you uncomfortable, but it also makes you observant. It slows you down just enough to notice the way someone gently makes space in a crowded elevator, or how a stranger offers their umbrella without drama.

Before, I used to admire the polished.
Now, I admire the people who stay kind when the weather is not.
