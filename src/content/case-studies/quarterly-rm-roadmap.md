sections:
  - heading: Context
    paragraphs:
      - primary: FundsIndia's platform grew up retail-first and mutual-fund-first. Private Wealth serves HNI clients through relationship managers, many of whom were new to the system.
  - heading: The problem
    paragraphs:
      - primary: Relationship-manager pain sat across the whole client lifecycle, and there was no structured way to rank it. In focus groups, managers said AIF and PMS-only clients could not be onboarded because a mutual-fund account was mandatory, and one corporate account had taken about 20 days to open. They also raised paper NRI tax declarations, and SIPs that could not be paused or changed.
  - heading: What I believed
    items:
      - primary: Relationship managers are the product's main users and its distribution. What they can't do, clients never see.
      - primary: Feedback only keeps coming if people see it acted on.
      - primary: Problems cluster by lifecycle stage. Grouping by stage makes trade-offs visible, instead of arguing item by item.
  - heading: Options, and what I didn't do
    items:
      - primary: I did not work down the list. I mapped issues to four themes. Onboarding, Core Investment Journeys, Reports and Servicing, and Enabling and Learning.
      - primary: Onboarding came first, with login for AIF and PMS-only clients as priority 0.
      - primary: I treated that login as the item that blocked revenue outright. The other items only slowed it down.
        pending: true
      - primary: I committed to a quarter, not a wish list. Only a subset made the cut. The rest was shown openly as queued.
      - primary: I tied the quarter to measurable targets, including onboarding time, query resolution time and platform-adoption query volume.
  - heading: Decision, and how it was tested
    items:
      - primary: I followed up on the focus groups and took ownership of re-examining the SIP lock rule with compliance and tech.
      - primary: I built the quarterly Private Wealth roadmap with supporting data and competitor onboarding benchmarks, then tested the priorities in a relationship-manager feedback session on 18 March 2026.
      - primary: I started a monthly Private Wealth product newsletter to close the loop. I designed the format. Product Ops and Marketing produced it.
  - heading: Result
    items:
      - primary: Of 45 relationship-manager feedback items, 19 made the quarter. 6 shipped in July and 13 were queued for August–September.
        fallback: About 40% of relationship-manager feedback made the quarter.
        pending: true
      - primary: The first newsletter reached 663 inboxes, with 395 unique opens, about a 60% open rate.
        fallback: About a 60% open rate on the first edition.
        pending: true
      - primary: The Advisor Productivity pillar was 85% complete by the late-May review. Onboarding Status on the relationship-manager dashboard reached 45% daily adoption.
        fallback: Roughly half of relationship managers checked Onboarding Status daily.
        pending: true
  - heading: What I'd do next
    items:
      - primary: Report outcomes, not just items shipped. Onboarding days and query volume each quarter.
      - primary: Add a lightweight impact score to each piece of feedback, so relationship managers can see why something was or wasn't picked.
